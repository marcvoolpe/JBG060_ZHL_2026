# Handoff: croplands × conflict EDA and research-objective search

Context document for starting a new chat. Covers what was built, what was found, what external reviewers concluded, and where the project stands on choosing a research objective (RO).

## 1. Project in one paragraph

JBG060 capstone (TU/e), stakeholders **ZOA** (Dutch NGO, agriculture/livelihoods) and **ZHL**, country **South Sudan**, spatial unit **county (admin2, ~78 counties)**. The team reuses the existing INFLOW-AI flood prediction model (see below) rather than building a better one. My branch was a multi-stage exploratory data analysis (EDA) on **croplands and conflict** to find a defensible, impact-focused RO linked to anticipatory action (AA). RO requirements are in `good_research_objective.txt` (Action, Outcome, Scope, Inputs, Evaluation with baseline + metrics, Purpose/stakeholder decision).

**User preference going forward:** be sceptical, not optimistic. Discard findings that aren't backed by proper data or don't have a real application. "Floods displace people" and similar known facts are not insights.

### Flood model the project relies on (INFLOW-AI)

- **Output:** flood prediction per **252 × 252 m pixel per dekad** (10-day period).
- **Lead time:** predictions are issued **2 months (6 dekads) in advance**.
- **Unusual floods:** predicted with an **F-score of 0.86**.
- **Recurring floods:** the model's F-score is only **0.55**, because recurring-flood pixels cover on average just ~0.1% of the territory per dekad. The plan is therefore **not** to rely on the model for them: **all recurring-flood pixels are treated as targets for aid** by default.

## 4. Findings that held up

- **Floods don't explain harvest loss.** Annual unusual flood vs FEWS harvested area within counties: null under every specification (SA-H1 falsified).
- **Harvest-window flood signal is fragile.** p = 0.017 with fixed windows, p = 0.12 with rain-defined windows, and exposed to the dry-season detection artefact. Not usable for loss claims.
- **Upstream hydrology gives no useful lead.** Best Spearman ≈ 0.21 (Dartmouth 1541, lag 3); county precip ≈ 0.27. Forecast lead-time narrative cut.
- **Floods hit rangeland more than cropland.** Unusual inundation ≈ 87% rangeland, 13% cropland (Session B).
- **Displacement tracks flooding** (SC-H2). Disaster-tagged DTM sites median 2.5 km from unusual flood vs 5.3 km for conflict-tagged; county disaster share vs unusual extent Spearman 0.62. This is a sanity check on the flood masks, not a new insight.
- **Flood–conflict link is only tentative** (SC-H1). Causal flood → conflict was cut.
- **Assessment bias is real.** Whether OCHA assessed a county relates to flood-exposed population (logit p = 0.001), so missing counties aren't random.
- **Exposure layers don't beat raw flood extent against observed impact.** Same target, OCHA people affected, n = 36, Spearman: raw unusual extent 0.33, WorldPop-exposed population 0.006, composite score −0.23, crop-exposed −0.39. Only a hint (n = 36, one snapshot, plus the sampling flaw in section 6).



## 5. What was rejected and why

User critique of the Session D figures:

- **Fig 1 (impact target):** looks patternless. The target was an exposure-composite tercile, and composite indices can hide severity (compensatory dilution, per external research).
- **Oracle false alarms:** the "oracle" was flood extent, but the target was the exposure composite, so even perfect flood knowledge misses.
- **Fig 3 (cost–loss):** C/L ratios were assumed, not sourced. The 6–7× dry-vs-wet transport premium isn't a universal parameter. With an upper-tercile target (~33% base rate), high hit rates mostly reflect firing often. Oracle net value (185.3) barely exceeds climatology (174.5), so a perfect forecast adds little over "the same places always flood."
- **Fig 4 (conflict overlay):** with a median of ~2 events per county, most counties have no usable conflict signal; "14 compound priority counties" isn't reliable.
- **Fig 5 (decision window):** the flood-detection peak falls in the dry season (artefact), which contradicts the claimed May–June decision window.

Three external deep-research reviews (`external_deep_research/`) judged the "exposure-coupled county ranking / decision-support tool" idea:

- **Already exists.** WFP ADAM (flood × population × cropland at admin2), Flood-PROOFS East Africa (separate population, cropland, grazing, livestock, road exposure), REACH / World Bank chronic flood maps. INFLOW-AI is already operational inside an existing AA protocol.
- **Wrong lead time.** Lake storage and Sudd transit (~17 months) don't fit a 2–3 month seasonal trigger.
- **Wrong decision.** NGOs don't re-rank counties every year; their footprints are stable over multi-year cycles. Dry-season pre-positioning is already standard practice.
- **Biased validation.** Checking against DTM/OCHA is exposed to access and reporting bias.
- **The gap they left:** nobody validates **exposure → observed impact** (people affected, displacement, crop/livestock loss) in South Sudan. Judge 1 suggested: how much does county impact vary year to year after controlling for persistent flood susceptibility, and do cropland/population layers improve prediction over flood persistence alone?



## 6. Known flaw in our exposure measure

`flood_exposed_pop` (`impact_session_a.py`, around line 456) reads WorldPop with `src.sample(coords)` at one point per flood pixel. WorldPop cells are ~92 m and flood pixels ~230 m, so each reading covers ~1/6 of the pixel's area: totals are undercounted ~6× and very noisy in sparse counties. The same point-sampling pattern is used for ASAP in `impact_session_b.py` (around lines 91–95), though a % cover mask is less affected. Fix: sum WorldPop cells inside each flood pixel footprint (zonal sum) before drawing any conclusion about exposure.

## 7. Where the RO search stands

**Conclusion so far:** croplands and conflict should **not** be the core of the RO with current data. Crop impact isn't detectable, conflict data is too sparse, and the one robust result (displacement near floods) isn't new. What would change this is new data only: NDVI/phenology (crop calendar test), real ACLED (communal violence, cattle raiding), recent livestock (AGLW; floods mainly hit rangeland, so the pastoral route is more plausible than crops). That would be a new, uncertain project.

**Useful framing for ZOA:** "satellite flood maps plus a static cropland mask cannot estimate crop impact in South Sudan" is itself a finding; the EDA justifies narrowing the question.
